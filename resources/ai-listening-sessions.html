Before you train staff on AI, listen first

By Keri Zeller, Founder

Many schools start AI training with a tool demonstration. Staff nod along, and very little changes. In my experience the problem is rarely the tool. It is that the training did not start with what people already know, already use, and already worry about.

When I served on an AI policy committee at an international school, we began with listening sessions instead. What we heard shaped both the policy and the department-specific training that followed.

Run the session in three parts

  • Experience. What AI tools have you tried, for work or at home? What happened?
  • Understanding. What do you think these tools do well, and where do they go wrong?
  • Feelings and concerns. What worries you about AI in your subject or with your students?

Keep groups small, by department if you can. Ask a colleague, not a senior leader, to take notes, and do not attach names to comments.

Turn what you hear into training

Sort the notes into three piles: skills people want, misunderstandings to correct, and concerns the policy must answer. Then design training by department, because a science teacher and a language teacher will use AI in very different ways.

A quick test: if a teacher reads your training agenda, can they see their own question in it? If not, go back to the notes.

Close the loop

Tell staff what you heard and what you changed because of it. That one step builds more trust in an AI policy than any slide about responsible use.
