Building an L&D function from scratch

By Keri Zeller, Founder

When I founded the training and development department at a university, there was no function at all: no calendar, no record of who had been trained, and no shared idea of what good looked like. More than 175 faculty and 40 support staff were waiting. Here is the order I would follow again in any organisation.

1. Listen before you build

Run a short needs assessment with leaders and staff. Ask what people struggle with, what they wish they had been taught when they started, and what gets in the way of good work. You will find that some problems are not training problems at all.

2. Fix the must-haves

Make a list of mandatory training and start tracking it. It is unglamorous, but it builds trust with leadership and protects the organisation.

3. Agree what good looks like

A simple competency framework, even one page per role, gives every later decision a reference point: what to train, who needs it, and how you know it worked.

4. Plan the year

Publish an annual training calendar. People plan around it, managers can release staff, and you stop running everything as an emergency.

5. Choose tools last

Set up the LMS once you know what it must hold. Start with courses, a catalogue, and completion reports. Add features when someone needs them.

6. Show what changed

Report more than attendance. Short follow-up surveys, manager check-ins, and a few concrete examples of changed practice tell leadership far more.

The short version: listen, cover compliance, define good, plan the year, then add tools.
