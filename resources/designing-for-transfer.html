Designing workshops for transfer

By Keri Zeller, Founder

A good workshop gets positive feedback forms. A useful workshop changes what people do the following week. These are not the same thing, and the difference is mostly decided before the session begins.

Before the session

  • Write the outcome as an action: what will participants do differently, and where?
  • Ask leaders what support and time they will give for practice afterwards.
  • Collect real examples from participants' own work to use in the session.

During the session

  • Spend at least half the time on practice with real tasks, not on slides.
  • End with each person writing a specific plan: what, with whom, and by when.
  • Give a tool they will keep using, such as a template, checklist, or protocol.

After the session

  • Schedule a short check-in two to three weeks later.
  • Ask for one piece of evidence of the new practice, such as a lesson plan or a student sample.
  • Share examples that worked so others can borrow them.
The 70-20-10 reminder: most learning happens on the job and through colleagues. Design the workshop to start that learning, not to contain it.
