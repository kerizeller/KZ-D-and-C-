Making feedback useful to students

By Keri Zeller, Founder

Teachers spend hours writing feedback that students glance at for a few seconds. The fix is rarely more feedback. It is a routine that makes feedback usable. Here are five checks I use with teachers in assessment and feedback workshops.

1. Is it tied to a clear goal?

Students should be able to see the success criteria before they start, and the feedback should point back to them.

2. Is there one next step?

Three comments a student acts on are worth more than ten they skim. Lead with the single most useful change.

3. Is there time to act on it?

If feedback arrives after the unit has moved on, it becomes a grade explanation. Build in lesson time to revise.

4. Does the student do the thinking?

Try a question or a marked area ("Something in this paragraph weakens your argument. Find it.") instead of the correction.

5. Can you see whether it worked?

Compare the draft with the revision. If the change is not visible, adjust the routine, not just the comment.

Try it this week: pick one class, give only one next step per student, and give ten minutes in the next lesson to act on it.
